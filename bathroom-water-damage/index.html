Bathroom Water Damage in Hammond

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Hammond, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

What It Looks Like

  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Rot Under the Toilet

Each flush leaks a small amount once the wax ring fails. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

A Failed Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Dry It or Replace It?

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.
