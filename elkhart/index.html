What to Do Before Our Elkhart Crew Arrives

The hour between finding water and the crew arriving is when a homeowner in Elkhart can do the most good. Look after people first and property second. Keep out of water that touches sockets, appliances or wiring, and only cut the power if the breaker box is in a dry place you can get to safely. Water Damage We See Most in Elkhart

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Elkhart households. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in Elkhart. Clean water from the tank or supply is one thing, but water from the bowl after use is contaminated. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Elkhart homes is well along before a stain appears. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.

How Elkhart Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in Elkhart, and they are worth learning. Paint that bubbles or peels, a brown ring on a ceiling, baseboards pulling away from the wall, and flooring that cups or lifts all mean moisture behind the surface. A musty smell in a closet, a toilet that rocks, or a tap that drips belongs on the list too.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

No amount of maintenance prevents every leak. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your Elkhart living room.
